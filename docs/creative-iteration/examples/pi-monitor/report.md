# Pi + old monitor: report

## What happened
1. I took your confirmed brief as given and wrote out the situation, the goal and eight places where your wants pull against each other.
2. Two quick agents answered the brief cold, with no help. One said "build a RetroPie couch arcade with a soldered button panel." That became the obvious answer to measure against.
3. Two agents looked for the real problem underneath, one using three randomly drawn thinking moves and one without. Both put "the last build died because you couldn't see why it didn't work" first. I wrote one framing paragraph from what they found.
4. One agent fetched 86 real precedents from 27 published pages: slow TV, teletext, Nam June Paik, shadow theatre, karaoke, Italian design-award winners, Chilean cloth art, Indian jugaad and more. A script dealt out slices so I never chose who saw what.
5. Six agents wrote 31 sketches. Four had random moves and constraints, one had none, and one was a single strange "wild card" sketch.
6. A fresh judge scored all 31 plus the two cold answers, shuffled and unlabeled. A checker then looked up every menu item, and one of them got swapped out.
7. **You picked** Patchwork Booth, Seven-hour line and Solder-along table, and noticed that your tag-board story was pulling the agents.
8. Three agents developed your picks and checked their sources. A fourth tried to kill all three and called each one fatal, each time for want of a fact about you.
9. **You chose Patchwork Booth** and supplied the missing fact: your kids.
10. A fact-checker went through the final write-up. It found one wrong claim and three unflagged guesses, and I fixed or marked all four.

## What you gave me
- **The ask:** a Pi 4, a 2014 24-inch monitor with a dead stand, a cheap USB mic, a bench of through-hole parts, a multimeter, a Bambu H2S with no CAD, $100, and up to six months of weekends.
- **The why:** something still plugged in and used six months after it's finished, not a toy.
- **Your no list:** smart-home dashboards, weather, family schedules, fitness tracking.
- **The setting:** surprise me.
- **At the menu:** your three picks, and the point about the tag-board story.
- **At the end:** "it has appeal to me and would also be a hit with my kids."

## The winner and the runners-up
- **Patchwork Booth (the choice).**
  - **What it is:** a cigar box with one soldered button and a ring LED in front of the monitor. Press it and talk for a minute, and your story becomes a stitched patch on a quilt that grows on screen. Press again and a random old story plays back.
  - **What it takes:** it's small, one script and a $5 button, and its life comes from the people who use it. That's exactly why the attack called it fatal until you said the kids would be the ones pressing it.
- **Seven-hour line.**
  - **What it is:** the monitor becomes a dark box of scent chambers that plays the Bergen-to-Oslo train run as smell over a workday. Reaching the end of the line ends work.
  - **The catch:** it's the most original of the three, but it lives on refills, your nose stops noticing within hours, and it doesn't use the monitor as a monitor. A cardboard-box workday test would settle it.
- **Solder-along table.**
  - **What it is:** the monitor lies flat under acrylic, shows your board at true scale, lights the next pad and shows the voltage your meter should read.
  - **The catch:** it's a tool you only use while building, and the glow may land a pad off.

## How each differs from the obvious answer
- **Patchwork Booth: stands out.** It's not a game, and the screen holds your family's voices instead of software. It's still a screen in the living room, though, which is a little closer to home than the other two.
- **Seven-hour line: stands out.** There's no screen at all, and it serves a different need: ending the workday.
- **Solder-along table: stands out,** but it's the kind of answer your tag-board story invited. It solves the old failure rather than serving the new why.

## What got filtered out
- **One idea was dead:** a petting lawn of living turf inside the monitor shell.
- **Two didn't fit:** a self-watching TV-Buddha sculpture, and the second cold answer, a generic appliance that never says what it does.
- **Duplicates got folded together.** Four scent-in-a-gutted-monitor ideas, two wake-up ideas, two writing terminals, two shadow stages and two wall-colour pieces each became one.
- **One menu item was swapped out:** "Unequal Window" (your own slow footage as a window) rested on a Hong Kong slow-TV broadcast the checker couldn't find. The next well took its place.
- **Below the menu cut:** Station Map Radio, Jam Partner, Bird Café and others. They were fine ideas at lower distance. Everything is in `vault.md`.

## Would another run land here?
- **Stable:**
  - The four answers every agent reached for on its own: an arcade, a smart mirror or dashboard, a photo or art frame, and a media centre. All five generators named all four.
  - "Debugging is the real problem": both problem agents, both cold answers, and two bench-tool sketches got there.
  - Scent in the gutted shell: one generator, four sketches, but forced by a card's "smell and touch only" constraint.
  - Kamishibai-style story stages: two agents, independently.
- **Fragile:**
  - **The winner came from one sketch by one generator.** It was shaped by one card (the Chilean arpilleras) and one constraint (a first version in four hours). It reached you through a wild slot, not the main list. Another run could well miss it.
  - **The kids premise came from you at Gate 2,** not from the brief.

## How good the brief was: 8/10
It had plenty of room, a clear no list and real limits.
- **Two gaps mattered.** It never said who else lives with you, and that turned out to decide the winner. It also never said how often you build, which decided the Solder-along table.
- **The "what killed the last one" answer was the strongest pull on the run.** Both problem agents made it problem one. I then wrote it into the framing as a principle ("hand-built parts whose faults can be found step by step with a multimeter"), which turned your story into a requirement for every later agent. Most sketches justified themselves as "multimeter-testable", and two made debugging the whole point.
- **Feature or bug?** It's a good filter for buildability. But a story about past failure tends to come back as a job to fix. That pull came partly from the question and partly from my framing.
