# Problems
Draws (stage `problems`): sci-zewail-strobe-the-fast, rev-spirit-line, mudaraba-share-the-outcome (seed: "Space Music (album)", Eloy Fritsch).

## Control (no cards), verbatim summary
1. **The last project died at the first failed power-on.** The real failure mode is "it didn't work and I had no way to find out why." Tension: building is part of the enjoyment, but every hand-built circuit is a chance to relive 2021.
2. **"Finished and never tinkered with" fights every idea that stays useful for six months.** Things stay in use because they keep adapting; the no list (dashboards, weather, schedule, fitness) reads like things that need feeding. A finished thing's content has to come from the world, not the person. Tension: finished and unchanging vs. alive and worth looking at in month six.
3. **The monitor and Pi are assets with no job, and "surprise me" has no steer.** It's easy to over-fit to the screen (as the arcade does) or to ignore it and waste the one big free asset. Tension: using the screen is cheap but pulls toward display-shaped ideas.
- Open question: is the finished thing something they look at, something they use with hands or voice, or something that mostly runs unseen?

## Problem agent (cards), verbatim summary
1. **Zewail (sample faster than the change): the brief never says how a failing circuit gets debugged.** A multimeter can't watch an audio signal; an agent can only reason about what can be measured, so "I have an agent now" doesn't fix the blindness. Tension: the Pi, mic and monitor could be a crude scope, but building that tool is tinkering.
2. **Spirit line (keep one line open): "complete as is" is defined as sealed, but things used for six months must change.** The no list shows how these things die: data, household or interests shift and a sealed object goes stale. Tension: finished vs. alive; the open line has to be one deliberate, low-effort channel, not general openness.
3. **Mudaraba (payment moves with outcome): all the cost is paid up front and the payoff is only known later.** $100 and six months of weekends are fixed charges against a binary test about a year away. Tension: staged commitment protects him but risks a half-built feel.
- Open question: what does the person already do every day, at a fixed time and place, that this could fold into?
- **Lead's note for the post-mortem:** this agent called the person by name, though the brief block never names them. The name came from inherited context, not the brief (a rule 6 leak; it changed nothing in the substance).

## Framing
The real problem isn't finding a use for a spare screen. It's making one finished thing that earns a place in the person's ordinary days and is still in use six months after the build ends, without them having to keep feeding it, fixing it or adding to it. A good idea honors four things. It is complete as is, with at most one deliberate opening for extension rather than open-ended scope. Its hand-built parts are ones whose faults can be found step by step with a multimeter and an agent's help. Its use comes from something the person already does or wants, not from novelty that wears off. And it puts the Pi and the old monitor to real work without assuming the monitor's job is showing information. The end product matters more than the build, but the build should be enjoyable.
